What is due

Intervals run on time and on distance, whichever arrives first. A car that sits still still needs its oil changed.

Authority

What needs you

You draw the line once. Everything under it gets handled and logged; everything over it waits here. Nothing is spent and no upgrade is fitted without crossing this page or your own rule.

Your limits

Set per collection, per car, or per shop. The trades never see the numbers — a painter who knows the ceiling writes an estimate just under it.

No rule written for something? It comes to you. That is the default, and it is the only default that cannot be wrong.

Interest

Offers

A car being shown is not a car for sale. But people ask — so let them ask properly, in writing, with a name attached, and on your terms. Every car is closed until you open it. Once it is open, the offer button rides along with the car wherever you send it — to another collector here, to a link, to a post — and every offer tells you which share brought it in.

Who may ask, and about what

Set per car. Nobody sees a number you have not published, and nothing below your floor ever reaches this page.

No setting written for a car means closed. An offer is an expression of interest, never a contract — accepting one here starts a conversation, it does not sell a car.

When a car leaves, the file goes with it

Sell it and the whole history lands in the buyer's account — every service, every receipt, every photograph, and the list of everyone who owned it before him. He does not need an account first; the car's file is what gives him one. What you paid, what you were offered and what you thought it was worth stay with you.

Goes to the buyer

  • Every service, repair, detail and paint entry
  • Receipts and invoices for that work, amounts included
  • Title, registration, build sheet, window sticker
  • Photographs and completed shot sets
  • Upgrades and their documents
  • Mileage history and the service schedule
  • The chain of owners before him

Stays with you

  • What you paid for the car
  • Your valuations
  • Every offer it drew and what you turned down
  • Your limits and who you gave access to
  • Anything about your other cars

Hold a document back if you want to — the buyer is told that something was withheld and how many, never what. A file with declared gaps is still honest. A file with quiet ones is not.

What this is

  • The demo collection, rendered so it can be judged before anyone writes code. Three surfaces over one record: the collection, the caretaker's desk and the shop.
  • The photographs are placeholders, and they are licensed. Eight openly-licensed pictures from Wikimedia Commons, credited below. They are not these cars — they are cars of the right year and model standing in for a record. In the real product every picture is Bob's own car, shot by his photographer against the shot list the app hands him.

  • Approvals: the owner sets the limits, the shop can only request, and work over the line cannot be logged until it is approved. Enforced in the data layer, not on a screen.
  • Offers: every item is closed until the owner opens it, offers carry a real name, anything under the floor never reaches him, and nothing here is a contract. Legal review before this surface ships.
  • Share and offer are one motion. Send an item to another member, to a link, or to a public post, and the offer control goes with it — with the friction scaled to how public the share is. The record always names which share produced the offer.
  • The record is an item, not a car. Vehicles first because that is the collection in front of us; the schema does not care what the item is.
  • Every record here is invented. The owner, the providers, the VINs, the histories and the valuations are fabricated, and the VINs are deliberately non-conforming so they cannot be mistaken for real vehicles.
